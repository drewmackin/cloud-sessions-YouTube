## What counts as proof here

Your rule was: a claim counts if it was said or sent by Kevin, a coach, or a member who has shown wins. Every item in this report carries one of these:

- <span class="tag v">Said on camera</span> the exact words from YouTube's captions of Kevin's own videos, with a clickable timestamp. Click it and you hear the line.
- <span class="tag v">Their document</span> the full text of a Google Doc owned by Kevin's Google account (confirmed through Drive's owner field) and linked in his own video descriptions. One of them was written by coach Elias for the group.
- <span class="tag v">Their description</span> the exact text of Kevin's own video descriptions as the search index returned them: the links he posts (Google Docs, the application form) and their tracking codes.
- <span class="tag c">Revenue</span> the revenue a coach or member claims on camera, usually with a Shopify dashboard shown. These are their own figures. Nobody audited them, and one doesn't match its own dashboard (see section 10).

## How “word for word” was guaranteed

1. **Spoken prompts** are cut by script from the caption text that Perplexity's index returned for each video. They are never retyped. Each cut runs from an exact first phrase to an exact last phrase inside one continuous caption window, and the build stops if a single word differs. Only the quotation marks the captions put around the speech are dropped, at the two ends.
2. **Documents** are printed in full from Google Drive's plain-text export, unchanged: emoji, capitals, typos and Markdown symbols included. The only layout change is that runs of empty lines are collapsed to one.
3. **Captions are captions.** “[ __ ]” is YouTube's own mask over a swear word. YouTube's speech-to-text writes “cloud” for Claude, “Higsfield” or “Hicksfield” for Higgsfield, “C dance” or “CDN” for Seedance, and “cling” for Kling. These are left exactly as captioned, with the correct name noted beside them. Where two caption versions of the same moment exist, the one that spells the tool correctly is used.

## Coverage, honestly

- **Window:** April 27 to October 27, 2026 (the last six months). Older material is used only where it is the source of something they still teach, and is labelled as such.

Section 7A rebuilds everything that has been published from inside it. Most videos came back as 25–70% of their transcript. Prompts that were shown only on screen and never spoken can't be captured from captions. The “What we could not get” list in section 10 names each missing piece and where it lives.

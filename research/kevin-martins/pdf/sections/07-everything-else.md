## Risks they don't talk about (our analysis, sourced)

1. **Undisclosed AI on TikTok.** TikTok's Community Guidelines require creators to label AI-generated or significantly edited content that shows realistic people or scenes. TikTok also auto-labels content it detects, and that label can't be removed ([TikTok support](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/ai-generated-content); [Integrity & Authenticity guidelines](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)). Mixing in real clips “so we don't get called out” doesn't remove the duty to label, and unlabeled realistic AI can be removed or restricted.
2. **Fake testimonials under the FTC rule.** Since October 21, 2024, 16 CFR Part 465 bans testimonials attributed to people who don't exist, which covers AI-generated ones. Courts can impose civil penalties for knowing violations ([FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials); [FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)). An AI avatar saying “I bought this for my sister and now she uses it every day” to US buyers is the exposure point. AI “doctors” promoting supplements add health-claim risk on top.
3. **Meta's unoriginal-content penalty.** Meta cuts distribution and monetization for accounts that repeatedly repost others' content without meaningful enhancement, and reduces reach on detected duplicates ([Meta creators blog](https://creators.facebook.com/blog/combating-unoriginal-content); [Meta, March 2026](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/03/rewarding-original-creators-on-facebook/)). One-for-one reposting and ripped showcase clips are exactly what this targets, even when the background is swapped.
4. **The “I own @video_1” line.** Elias's Seedance template makes you state ownership of the source video. Kevin describes the method as a way {{q:Hgc13jXWm_c|to literally steal a clip,|the entire environment}} and tells members to mute clips first because otherwise Higgsfield will {{q:Hgc13jXWm_c|block the generation due to copyright.|due to copyright.}} Muting to dodge a copyright filter, plus a false ownership statement, is the riskiest step in the stack, both for the platform account and legally.
5. **Look-alike faces.** Pinterest photos of real people turned into “similar facial features” avatars can still raise right-of-publicity claims if the person is recognizable. Kevin's own line is “that's how you get sued”.
6. **Real retailers in fake scenes.** Run twos set in Costco, Target or IKEA, ending with “just kidding, we sell them”, imply a retail presence that doesn't exist. That brings trademark and false-advertising exposure the more the brand is shown.
7. **Shared prompts mean shared fingerprints.** When a whole cohort runs the same coach prompt (the 321 prompt, the Seedance brief), the outputs converge. Charlie saw about 30 accounts copy his winner and none of them went viral: {{q:S9COXhnjijY|everyone was just one-on-one posting my video,|not how organic works}} The prompt is not a moat. Speed, niching and testing are.
8. **Tool churn.** The stack changed from Sora 2/Veo to Higgsfield, Kling and Seedance within a year. Budget for re-learning, and keep the method separate from any one tool.

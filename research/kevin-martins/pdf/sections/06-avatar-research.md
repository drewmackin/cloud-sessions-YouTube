<div class="note"><b>Key idea:</b> in this group the “avatar” is the on-screen AI person, and that person is built to <b>look like the buyer</b>. So customer research comes first and directly shapes the face, age, clothes and setting. {{q:hK2Os3wSPI8|people want to buy from other people that kind of look like them|that resonate with them}}</div>

## Step 1: research the customer (ICP)

1. **Run the Niche Research Prompt (P1) in Claude** with your market, product, link and one-line benefit. It returns 12 sections: market overview, demographics, psychographics, hobbies, pains, desires, emotional buying triggers, objections, how they talk, where they hang out, which hooks work on them, and the top 3 content angles, then a 3-line summary to pin. Kevin's instruction: {{q:OTRP8hk1wr4|ask Claude to profile our ideal customer,|what emotionally drives them to buy.}}
2. **Pick buyers who have both the pain and the money.** Kevin's flop: {{q:WVzIUxovPdA|I was targeting a terrible audience,|teenage girls with no money,}} Omar's win: {{q:WVzIUxovPdA|he was targeting a perfect ICP|40-year-old men and above that are suffering with balding,}}
3. **Niche it to a passion or identity.** Elias's Lord of the Rings lamp: {{q:WVzIUxovPdA|the product is perfectly niched|to the Lord of the Ring audience,}} Same logic for sub-niches: niche down from “bookworms” to one genre or even one book.
4. **Keep the research in the chat.** Claude uses the same conversation for the avatar image (P2), the copy and the hooks: {{q:OTRP8hk1wr4|this will be the foundation of, you know, generating our avatar,|making our videos, etc.}}

## Step 2: build the AI avatar (the person on screen)

| Route | How | Proof |
|---|---|---|
| **From the research** | In the same Claude chat, ask for an image prompt of the buyer (P2), then render it in GPT Image 2 inside Higgsfield | Kevin, “healthmaxxing” video: the first avatar, a balding man, for Omar's red-light hat |
| **From Pinterest** (Kevin's “lazy” route) | Search a niche term (“Japanese mechanic”), pick a face and setting that fit, then have Claude write a look-alike prompt (P5) | {{q:hK2Os3wSPI8|go on Pinterest and try to find reference images|that matches your niche.}} |
| **Unique faces** | Avoid the generic “AI face”. Hunt for distinctive real-looking people on Pinterest and test several avatars | Gus ($60K): {{q:kmy9uOj0ve0|Use Pinterest, bro.|you can find some unique looking people,}} |
| **Role-matched** | Pick the job or role the buyer trusts or relates to: delivery and construction workers for hats, a doctor for health | Omar: {{q:putfs1jofwo|I was in Pinterest looking for avatars,|I was looking for a doctor.}} |
| **Market-matched** | An avatar and background that read as the target country, so views come from buyers in the right market | John ($65K): {{q:FAtJ50Kh5-8|I'm just using my character that is so American|tailored to American people.}} |

**Then lock the character.** The master frame becomes the reference for every later shot, so the same person appears throughout. This is what Sora 2 couldn't do and Higgsfield's models can.

## Step 3: niche the scene, not just the face

- **Match the setting to the buyer:** an Initial-D-style garage for a car watch; Costco for older shoppers; Petco to call out dog owners. {{q:T6-LXSu0qHw|calling out like a direct ICP,|shopping at Petco,}}
- **Match the crowd too:** in run-two videos even the extras are niched (veterans rushing a veteran alarm clock).
- **Avatar types that print** (examples Kevin breaks down): the Amish health lady ({{q:GfhpQLqF658|this is going to be our 50K a month avatar.|our 50K a month avatar.}}, funnelling to an Amazon link), AI “doctors” for supplements, a goth avatar for emo buyers. {{q:FFPQYyPqQFI|emo people want to buy from other emo people,|Christian people want to buy from other Christian people.}}

## Step 4: test avatars like hooks

{{q:b3xKO_MABXg|creation process, even what your avatar is wearing,|you can split test it.}} Gus split-tested setting and avatar age as separate variables until one stuck, and Ivan built niche-research “prompt structures” in Claude (and Kimi) and found rooms on Pinterest.

<div class="warn"><b>The line Kevin draws himself:</b> {{q:hK2Os3wSPI8|we don't want to impersonate a real human being.|That's how you get sued.}} Their look-alike method still starts from a real person's Pinterest photo. “Similar facial features” is a judgement call, not a legal safe harbor (see section 7B, risks).</div>
